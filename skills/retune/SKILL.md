# RAMA: Retune

*Adapted from ghost-writer's `retune` (MIT, Simone Di Roma).*

Read `references/project-files.md` and `references/survey.md` first.

No critique and no restructuring. Only: make it sound like the author.

## Rules

- Don't evaluate the author's sample. It is a voice sample, not a draft.
- Never make structural changes. Content and order stay intact.
- The style guide still governs. A retune can't bring back a banned
  construction, even if the author's sample uses one. Point it out once and
  ask whether the guide should change.
- One pass at a time. Show the result and ask if it's closer.

## Phase 0: Chapter and sample

Ask which chapter (or passage) feels off, if it isn't given. Read it, the
style guide's voice and register sections, and the register samples.

Then:

> Show me how you'd say it. Don't try to write well. A few sentences, a dictated note, the worst paragraph rewritten your way. The rougher the better.

Wait, and don't suggest anything. If the author is stuck:

> Pick the paragraph that feels most wrong. Read it aloud, then say the same thing the way you'd tell it to someone across a table. Type that.

## Phase 1: Find the gap (internally)

Compare the draft with the sample: sentence length and rhythm, diction
(more literary or abstract than the author?), energy (where did the author
have heat that the draft flattened?), specificity (concrete details the
draft turned into abstractions), and distance (outside versus inside).

## Phase 2: Retune

Rewrite the chapter or passage with the same content, order and meaning.
Change only diction, rhythm, register, specificity and energy, toward the
sample. Use the author's own phrases verbatim where they fit.

## Phase 3: Show

Show the retuned text with no preamble. Then ask in a survey: **Closer:
keep it / Closer, but still off (I'll show you where) / Worse: go back.**
Iterate on whatever is still off, narrowing to the passages the author
points at.

## After a successful retune

1. Replace the chapter text in the manuscript and update its word count in
   the RAMA Log.
2. Under `## Voice Notes` in the RAMA Log (create the section after Decisions),
   record what the sample taught: phrases to use, rhythms, words the author
   never uses.
3. Offer each lasting lesson as a proposed line for the style guide, one at a
   time. Add each only on approval.

> Voice notes saved. `compose` will use them from the next chapter on.
